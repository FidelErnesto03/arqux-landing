From decision to result

What you agree on today should guide tomorrow's work.

ArqUX keeps project rules and decisions as references that can inform later tasks. Each task follows a reviewable path, with controls depending on the version and environment.

Example: "Do not show sensitive data in error messages".

Choose a stage to see who takes part, what goes in, what changes and what can be reviewed.

What is governed

Six questions. Clearer criteria for every task.

Who acts? What can they do? What information do they use? What was decided? What happened? What can be verified? ArqUX organizes these responsibilities beyond a single conversation.

They are six questions about the work a project lets its agents do; the selector is a navigation aid, not a promise of security. The internal architecture has other pieces —identity, context, processes, session, evidence and learning—: that is a different map, which you can explore in the artifacts.

How a criterion is incorporated

In the inspected identity.elevate path, a proposal can be reviewed in dry_run and applied after confirming the proposal hash (confirm_hash). Other paths depend on configured policies. Recording a lesson does not guarantee future application: the next task must have the relevant context available.

The same information can be precise and readable.

What was verified in the codec

The certificate figures refer to encoding and parity tests on codec artifacts within its declared corpus. They do not prove model comprehension or certify ArqUX security. Inside the format, an atom repeated three or more times is recorded as an alias; "token" here is a codec unit, not a model token — that equivalence is not proven by the certificate. The byte and parity figures belong to the codec_cortex 1.0.2-rc.1 certificate and cover the encoding of its own artifacts. See the evidence →

How agents receive this context

Tool independence

Governance belongs to the project, not the agent.

A project keeps its own rules and references. Agents may receive them through compatible instructions or MCP, depending on the integration. Incorporating lessons follows the applicable review or policy.

Commands, conditions and integration limits

Project instructions — sync-harnesses generates files for the four implemented conventions and drift-check detects differences when you run it.

MCP operations — arqux serve exposes the governance handlers over stdio; the configuration is external and belongs to each client (the pinned guide shows Cursor as an example). A client without MCP support can still adhere through files.

arqux init
arqux sync-harnesses
arqux drift-check
arqux serve

The four targets are declared in _TARGETS: harness.py · _TARGETS ↗ at 3903339. They describe projection formats, not the total of harnesses that can adhere.

No dedicated native projection or automatic MCP is claimed for any of them, and client configuration always requires a user step; a projection only helps if the client actually loads it, and switching tools preserves the project's references only when the integration receives and interprets them.
