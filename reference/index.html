Learning

A recorded lesson is not yet an active rule.

Lessons are recorded in the project brain, but recording is not approving and approving is not applying. Elevating a lesson to a criterion follows a defined route: the inspected path requires a dry-run proposal and hash confirmation; other routes can run under a configured policy. Mutations on protected elements may require explicit confirmation.

Lesson recorded→Review or policy→Criterion available
  • 01RecordA lesson is recorded in the project brain; it stays a finding, not a rule.
  • 02Review or promoteOne route requires a dry-run proposal and confirmed hash; another can run under configured policy.
  • 03ApplyOnly when the route reaches an available criterion does the rule govern the next work.

In the home example, the error-message decision could be recorded as a lesson; its incorporation would depend on the applicable route and review. The home page shows the decision, not its promotion.

CORTEX / HCORTEX

One structured source; two ways to read it.

CORTEX is the canonical representation of governed information; HCORTEX is its human reading. It is the same content with the same meaning: one source, two readings — not two stores.

Signal, not filler

One idea per line

Norms live in typed ideas, not in prose. Density is a property of the format, not a promise about comprehension.

Codec aliases

Repeats become aliases

An atom repeated three times or more is recorded as an alias inside the format. "Token" here is a codec unit, not a model token: that equivalence is not proven by the certificate.

Certificate scope

Same bytes everywhere

Within the certified scope — encoding parity of the codec's own artifacts — the same entry yields the same canonical bytes across runtimes. HCORTEX is a rendering, not a second source.

The byte and parity figures belong to the codec_cortex 1.0.2-rc.1 certificate and cover the encoding of its own artifacts; they do not prove model comprehension or general security. See the evidence →

Agnostic integration

ArqUX governs projects; it does not impose an agent.

Governance lives in project files and in handlers a client can expose over MCP. It does not depend on a model, a provider or a specific harness: an agent adheres by reading the context available in its environment, and the MCP connection depends on the client supporting and configuring it.

How an environment adheres

There are two complementary paths. By files: arqux init creates the governed workspace — the .arqux/ tree and the AGENTS.md kernel at the root — and arqux sync-harnesses projects that kernel into the native instruction files; arqux drift-check reports manual edits. By MCP: arqux serve exposes the handlers over stdio and each client configures it on its own.

Four files, one per instruction convention each environment reads natively. They are implemented projection targets — not a closed list of admitted agents.
