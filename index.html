For people who run projects where AI agents do the work

Your agents change.
Your projects evolve.
Your judgment endures.

ArqUX is a framework for governing your projects, built by AI agents. Each project keeps its own context, decisions and evidence — so work stays continuous, reviewable and auditable across agents, sessions and harnesses, and reviewed lessons can serve the rest of your workspace.

The problem

Context breaks in five ordinary ways.

Long-term work requires supervision and multiple iterations. Recovering the reasoning behind a result can take more work than recovering the result itself.

The output can survive. The criterion behind it, not always.

  1. Between specialised agentsInterpretation

    An analysis agent and a graphics agent exchange context as prose. The original reading drifts and the criteria take a different shape.

  2. A change of harnessReconstruction

    The next harness reads the project’s code and documentation from scratch just to begin.

  3. The context window runs outCompression

    Compression drops directives and guidelines. A summary is not enough; reading everything again exhausts the window again.

  4. Sharing a projectTransfer

    Handing over a project with its context makes the other person evaluate it all over, with room for a different reading.

  5. Closing the dayRecovery

    When the session or the app ends, the evolution so far — and the context that led there — is hard to recover.

THE HIDDEN COST OF LOSING CONTINUITY

You don’t just lose context. You lose some of the work you’ve already done.

Every time you change agent, tool or session without preserving the necessary references, you may have to reconstruct decisions your project had already resolved.

Cost 01 Your time Explaining requirements, decisions, constraints and background again. Cost 02 Tokens and processing Re-reading documents, reconstructing reasoning and processing known information again. Cost 03 Criteria drift Established decisions are reinterpreted. You have to spot the differences, correct them and reaffirm them. Cost 04 Rework Unnecessary changes, repeated solutions and new checks on results already accepted.

Cost 01 · Your time

When context is not available, you have to step in again to reconstruct the state of work. This can include explaining earlier decisions, locating information, recalling constraints, clarifying priorities and checking that the agent understood correctly.

ConsequenceTime you could spend on the next objective goes into recovering the previous one.

Cost 02 · Tokens and processing

A new agent may need to inspect files, conversations, code and documents that were already analysed. That reconstruction can trigger new model calls, consume input and output tokens, occupy context capacity and require additional processing.

ConsequencePart of the compute budget goes into rebuilding information that was previously available.

Cost 03 · Criteria drift

An agent that does not receive the relevant governance references may make decisions incompatible with previously approved constraints. You have to identify the divergence, recover the original rationale, restore the criterion and review the consequences of the drift.

ConsequenceThe project does not only lose time: it can move in a direction that contradicts its own decisions.

Illustrative example

“Do not expose sensitive data in error messages.”

New interpretation: another agent implements a debugging mechanism that logs sensitive information on certain errors.

Drift: the new solution contradicts the previously approved criterion.

Recovery cost: detect the contradiction, locate the original criterion, fix the implementation and repeat the affected checks.

You don’t just repeat an explanation. You may also have to undo work that should never have drifted.

Illustrative example, not a real execution of the framework.

Cost 04 · Rework

Missing references can lead an agent to investigate a solved problem again, reopen a closed decision or change a solution that already met the established criteria. Correcting it can take more work and new tests.

ConsequenceThe project spends effort again to recover a state it had already reached.

How costs accumulate

  1. Context change
  2. Reconstruction
  3. Possible drift
  4. Correction and review
  5. New progress

These costs can combine, but they do not necessarily occur on every session or tool change.

Cross-cutting consequenceWhen the basis of a decision is lost, reconstructing why it was taken, who intervened and what evidence supported it can be just as costly. Preserving information and preserving its justification are related problems, not the same problem.

How much of your effort goes into creating something new, and how much into reconstructing what you’ve already resolved?

Project governance shouldn’t depend on what a conversation remembers.

GOVERNANCE THAT OUTLIVES THE SESSION

Work changes.
Projects evolve.
Criteria, decisions and evidence remain — per project, across agents.

ArqUX organizes your project's rules, decisions, context and evidence into a governed context. Work can be resumed, reviewed and improved without relying solely on what a single conversation remembers.
